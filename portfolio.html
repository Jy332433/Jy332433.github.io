Low-dimensional Topology of Deep Neural Networks

Junyu Ren, Lek-Heng Lim

ICML 2026 · poster. arXiv · ICML page PDF

Overall
2.2
1 human-only3 partnership5 AI-led
breakdown

Width-matched feedforward networks with monotone activations cannot separate certain linked or knotted manifolds; skip connections, attention, and non-monotone activations enable the "folding" operations that break this topological barrier. We treat neural-network expressivity as a question of geometric transformation on embedded data, not only approximation rate or target-function fit. Experiments expose the gap as accuracy ceilings on Hopf-link tasks, and a linking-detection algorithm finds real-image linking in CIFAR-10 whose consistency matrix predicts pairwise classification difficulty better than standard distance metrics. The result positions low-dimensional, ambient-sensitive topology as a concrete lens on why modern architectures can transform data geometries that plain feedforward networks cannot.

Manifold-Weighted Neural Networks

Junyu Ren, Lek-Heng Lim

Under review. PDF

Overall
2.5
1 human-only3 partnership5 AI-led
breakdown

Universal approximation for neural networks whose weight matrices take values in matrix manifolds — orbits of classical Lie group actions in Rd×k, including Stiefel and Grassmann manifolds. We prove Lp(K)-universality for residual manifold-weighted networks with bounded scalar multipliers, via two sufficient condition sets — Register-Isolated Primitive Realizability and Cross-Axis Fold-and-Cut — verified case-by-case across an expanded manifold catalog.

Training a 2-token attention is NP-hard

Junyu Ren, Zehua Lai, Lek-Heng Lim

Under review. PDF

Overall
2.8
1 human-only3 partnership5 AI-led
breakdown

The modern attention-head analogue of Blum and Rivest's 1988 result that training a 3-node neural network is NP-complete. We show that for a single attention head with 1-dimensional query, key, and value, and n-dimensional token input — arguably the simplest attention head — the exact-fit decision problem on a training set of 2-token sequences is NP-hard; replacing softmax with hardmax tightens this to NP-completeness.

Optimization Dynamics Imprint Semantic Specificity in Contrastive Embedding Norms

Ziwei Su, Junyu Ren, Victor Veitch

Under review. PDF

Overall
2.3
1 human-only3 partnership5 AI-led
breakdown

Contrastive embedding models trained with scale-invariant losses (CLIP, SimCLR, Sentence-BERT) pair with cosine similarity and ignore magnitude. Yet the "discarded" embedding norms correlate with concept specificity, token frequency, and human uncertainty. We derive an analytic formula showing how embedding length encodes specificity as a byproduct of training optimization dynamics, turning norms into a free inference-time calibration signal for zero-shot classification and retrieval.

MAPPA: Scaling Multiagent Systems with Process Rewards

Ed Li†, Junyu Ren†, Cat Yan

ICLR 2026 Workshop RSI (Poster). OpenReview

Overall
2.8
1 human-only3 partnership5 AI-led
breakdown

Finetuning LLM-based multiagent systems faces two structural problems: credit assignment across agents, and sample efficiency of expensive multi-agent rollouts. MAPPA addresses both by assigning per-action process rewards from AI feedback — supervising each agent action rather than only the task outcome — without ground-truth labels. On unseen math problems, MAPPA reports +5.0–17.5 pp on AIME and +7.8–17.2 pp on AMC; on tool-augmented data analysis, success rate improves by +16.7 pp and quality metrics by up to 47%.

Agent-as-a-Coach: Towards Fully Agentic, Stateful, and Tool-Augmented Process Rewards

Ed Li†, Junyu Ren†, Cat Yan, Kerem Goksel

ICLR 2026 Workshop MALGAI. OpenReview

Overall
2.2
1 human-only3 partnership5 AI-led
breakdown

Existing LLM-as-a-Judge process-reward models are stateless: each evaluation is an independent call, with no memory of prior scores, no scoring-drift awareness, and no self-calibration mechanism — even as thousands of evaluations accumulate. Agent-as-a-Coach upgrades the judge into a tool-augmented LLM agent with persistent per-coach memory, rolling evaluation statistics, and a multi-turn reasoning loop: the coach queries its own scoring history, detects task-type biases, writes qualitative notes for self-calibration, and passes messages via external memory. On DSBench with a three-agent data-science pipeline (Qwen3-4B), the coach produces adaptive rebalancing between classification and regression tasks that stateless judges cannot generate.

AI safety / hallucination

EG-VAR: A Path Toward Eliminating LLM Hallucination in Empirical Inference via Tool-Attested Kernel Proofs

Junyu Ren

ICML 2026 Workshops FAGEN, TAIGR & PhilML — three parts of one agenda: proof-checker feedback for agentic workflows (FAGEN), formal sidecars as technical governance for high-stakes outputs (TAIGR), and how much world-directed reasoning can be made explicit in auditable formal commitments (PhilML, From Prompts to Proof Obligations). arXiv · FAGEN on OpenReview · TAIGR on OpenReview · PhilML on OpenReview · FAGEN (PDF) · TAIGR (PDF) · PhilML (PDF)

Overall
2.3
1 human-only3 partnership5 AI-led
breakdown

Tool access alone does not prevent two structural failures in LLM empirical reasoning: accepted outputs need not descend from attested evidence, and accepted deductions need not hold up under formal scrutiny. EG-VAR is a Lean 4-based tool-calling architecture where the kernel is the sole minter of verified claims via tool-attestation axioms — every verified output structurally descends from an attested tool call and a kernel-checked chain of valid inference; otherwise the system honestly abstains with a replayable audit trail. On TableBench (n=120), EG-VAR attains 120/120 versus a 95% same-tool baseline; on a 5-domain counterfactual stress panel it remains 100% source-faithful while same-tool drops to 80–90%.

Pure mathematics

Extensions of the Pierce–Birkhoff conjecture beyond hyperplane partitions

Junyu Ren, Zehua Lai, Lek-Heng Lim

In preparation, 2026.

Overall
4.0
1 human-only3 partnership5 AI-led
breakdown

Extensions of the Pierce–Birkhoff conjecture — every piecewise-polynomial function on Rn is a finite max–min combination of polynomials — from polyhedral (hyperplane) subdivisions to subdivisions induced by non-hyperplane partitions. Current status: an early ideal-level obstruction (where (z, x2) is strictly smaller than the chamber-intersection ideal (x, z)) has been sharpened. The quadratic n=3 case remains open; the workflow has produced a pathwise additive-separator reduction, then audited it into a three-part frontier — finite-separation reduction, exceptional-orthant absorption, and descended quadratic infinitely-near absorption.

Workflow remark. Semi-autonomous. Claude Code is framer and orchestrator: it prepares prompts, packages local context, records proof states, dispatches independent critiques, and turns sparse mathematical directions into structured exploration. Deliberate model mix — GPT Pro runs long proof attempts through the browser; Claude Code maintains the versioned research state and decides what to ask next; GPT-5.5/Codex (called by Claude Code) audits Claude's decisions and its interpretations of GPT Pro output adversarially yet constructively. The harness has found real local mechanisms, retracted overclaims, and left a precise list of mathematical bottlenecks.

Hodge-decomposed Hochschild cohomology of socle quotient rings

Junyu Ren, Lek-Heng Lim, Ke Ye

In preparation, 2026.

Overall
4.0
1 human-only3 partnership5 AI-led
breakdown

Hochschild and Harrison cohomology of socle quotient rings Rf = T/(f), in a long-running computation-driven loop: symbolic-computation scripts expose patterns, the patterns suggest conjectures, the conjectures get turned into proofs or refuted. Currently at a paper-track GCT / orbit-separation package — several Landsberg-direction subcase separations, a structural obstruction theorem for low-degree Hochschild invariants, and verified closure computations at the (N, d) = (12, 3) Problem 6.18 test point.

Workflow remark. Claude Code orchestrates several loops at once: writes and revises Macaulay2 / Singular / SageMath scripts, prepares cluster jobs, parses and normalizes outputs, cross-validates computations across tools, turns tables into candidate closed forms, and keeps the proof-state file current. Persistent subagent roles — computation, representation theory, formula search, strategy — with separate review passes by fresh agents. All work runs under a proof-status taxonomy (proved, proved for N=k, verified from multiple independent fresh-context adversarial runs, cross-validated, conjectural, refuted, blocked, open); strongest labels require repeated clean adversarial-review rounds, and any issue resets the count.

Unlocking Hierarchical Concept Discovery in Language Models Through Geometric Regularization

Ed Li†, Junyu Ren†

ICLR 2025 Workshop BuildingTrust. OpenReview

Overall
2.0
1 human-only3 partnership5 AI-led
breakdown

Exponentially-Weighted Group Sparse Autoencoders (EWG-SAE): a geometrically decaying group-sparsity regularizer that resolves feature absorption in interpretable language-model analysis (e.g., "starts with S" being absorbed into "short"). The architecture partitions latent dimensions into K=9 groups with exponentially decaying L1 penalties (λk = λbase · 0.5k), reducing absorption while preserving reconstruction fidelity, sparse-probing score, and ℓ1 loss; negative inter-group correlations confirm hierarchical organization.

Routing-Mediated Structural Pseudo-Alignment in Sparse Mixture-of-Experts

Junyu Ren, Su Hyeong Lee, Risi Kondor

Mech Interp Workshop @ ICML 2026 (Poster). OpenReview PDF

Overall
3.7
1 human-only3 partnership5 AI-led
breakdown

Sparse mixture-of-experts language models route each input through a small discrete subset of experts. We identify an alignment-relevant failure mode invisible to expert-weight inspection: a small contamination of router parameters alone (experts frozen) can expose a pre-existing expert-local computation, redirecting safety-relevant inputs through experts whose routed-conditional objectives diverge from the global one — without any expert weight changing. We call this structural pseudo-alignment and validate the mechanism on OLMoE-1B-7B and DeepSeek-MoE-16B with three linked diagnostics.

When Do Transformer Components Compose? Validating a Log-Pool Decomposition Criterion

Junyu Ren, Su Hyeong Lee, Risi Kondor

PhilML @ ICML 2026 (Poster). OpenReview PDF

Overall
2.2
1 human-only3 partnership5 AI-led
breakdown

Does the log-pool compositional utility gap — a recently proposed criterion for decomposing transformer architectures into compositionally meaningful component sets — measure anything non-trivial when applied to trained models? We validate the gap along three axes (generalization, model/protocol variation, trained-structure sensitivity) at three resolutions (atomic, random partitions, genetic-algorithm-optimized partitions) across Llama 3.1 8B, Pythia 1.4B, and Gemma 2 2B. The gap discriminates trained transformer structure from parameter-scrambled controls in every cell, while leaving open whether the measured property reflects subagent structure, distributional coherence, or broader decomposition coherence.
